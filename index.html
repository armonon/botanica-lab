Guardrails

Claims and safety architecture

Language to use internally

  • “Target state” instead of disease treatment.
  • “Formulation hypothesis” instead of protocol.
  • “Candidate ingredient range needs review” instead of dosing instructions.
  • “Evidence quality: peer-reviewed / traditional / anecdotal / unknown.”

Language to avoid publicly

  • Prescription analogues such as “Adderall-like,” “Xanax-class,” or “MDMA-like.”
  • Claims to diagnose, treat, cure, prevent, or mitigate disease.
  • Guaranteed onset, guaranteed effect, or “works for everyone.”
  • Unreviewed contraindication or drug-interaction guidance.

Mandatory review flags

  • Mood, anxiety, sleep, hormones, pain, pregnancy, liver risk.
  • Anticoagulants, sedatives, stimulants, SSRIs/SNRIs/MAOIs, CYP interactions.
  • High-potency extracts, obscure botanicals, or stacked actives.
  • Any consumer-facing copy or formula launch decision.

Evidence anchors

Research notes powering the first pass

The full claims review is still open. These anchors are included so the site is transparent about where early assumptions came from.

FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance — advertising must be truthful, non-misleading, and substantiated by competent and reliable scientific evidence. FDA Questions & Answers on Dietary Supplements — supplements are not pre-approved by FDA before marketing; firms are responsible for lawful labeling and safety. Saffron mood evidence — 2026 GRADE-assessed systematic review/meta-analysis of 34 randomized controlled trials. Rhodiola fatigue evidence — 2012 systematic review on physical and mental fatigue. Kava safety evidence — systematic review focused on safety in anxiety studies; liver/sedation risk still requires conservative handling. L-theanine + caffeine attention literature — current RCT/meta-analysis trail for attention and mood in healthy participants.
